# Day 4 — Thursday, October 29, 2026

A short build day: recovery, verification, and getting the public
record caught up with the box.

**Cold-start proof.** The box had been powered down overnight. On
power-up it came back the way an appliance should: board serving, the
call transcription service running, the carrier trunk registered, the
on-screen keyboard and the Android session both up with their windows
present, and the board's data feeders pushing again. Nothing needed a
hand. That is the whole point of the appliance shape — the senior in
the demo story does not have a "server won't start" morning.

**Rules, settled.** A loose end from the kickoff: whether the
hackathon's Official Rules needed a separate acceptance click. The
rules text itself answers it — registering is the agreement ("By
registering to attend the Hackathon, you acknowledge that you have
read, understood, and agree to these terms"), and a signed waiver only
appears as a precondition for paying out prizes. Nothing separate is
owed; if the submission portal asks for anything at submit time, it
will surface there.

**This repository.** Days 2–4 landed in the sanitized package as three
commits with these notes: the appliance move and the phone Guardian's
first light (Day 2), the hold flow, the second phone, the text-fraud
call and the Fuse Shop (Day 3), and this catch-up (Day 4). The README
was corrected where the build had outrun it — the phone Guardian is
described as built now, not planned, and the Day 1 scareware catch is
worded as what it was: a live test on the owner's own machine. The
sanitization pass was re-run over every new file: no names, no real
phone numbers, no addresses, no account or claim numbers, no keys —
demo data and example addresses only, same as Day 1.

**Still ahead before the November 4 deadline:** the demo film, a real
senior (not the builder) testing the box on camera, the board's model
ticker (which Meta model is working, reflex vs. judgment), and the
final submission pass. The Windows-side proof of Add-a-PC from a real
Windows client is still open; the installer has only been verified
from inside the home network so far.
